# Project 1 Edition A - Automatic Data Processing (ADP)

## Decision and user

This analysis supports an initial-position decision by the fund's investment committee: initiate ADP, place it on the watchlist and defer purchase, or decline further work.

## Company and valuation date

- Company: Automatic Data Processing, Inc. (Nasdaq: ADP)
- Valuation date: September 1, 2026
- Reference price: $280.83 per share (ADP investor-relations quote, delayed at least 20 minutes)

## Initial thesis: watch-defer

ADP appears to be a high-quality, capital-light dividend compounder with recurring payroll and human-capital-management revenue, strong client retention, consistent organic growth, margin resilience, and net share repurchases. It fits the fund's desired business-quality profile.

I would not initiate at this stage. At $280.83, the shares trade at approximately 25.7 times fiscal 2026 diluted EPS of $10.94. That multiple may be reasonable for ADP's durability, but Edition A does not yet establish a sufficient margin of safety or an expected five-year return of at least 8%. The appropriate initial decision is therefore to place ADP on the research watchlist while completing valuation and downside work.

## Known evidence

Unless noted otherwise, dollar amounts are in millions and fiscal-year data are for the year ended June 30, 2026.

- ADP reported revenue of $21,947.4, up 7%; organic constant-currency revenue grew 6%.
- Diluted EPS was $10.94, up 10%. The weighted-average diluted share count declined to 403.3 million from 408.7 million in fiscal 2025.
- Earnings-before-income-taxes margin expanded 30 basis points; adjusted EBIT margin expanded 80 basis points.
- Employer Services new-business bookings grew 6%, client revenue retention was 92.1%, U.S. pays per control grew 1%, and PEO average worksite employees grew 2%.
- ADP returned $4.7 billion to shareholders: $2.6 billion in dividends and $2.1 billion in repurchases. It repurchased 8.6 million shares in fiscal 2026.
- Cash and cash equivalents were $4,230.1 at June 30, 2026. Current debt was $1.0 and noncurrent long-term debt was $4,964.1. Client-funds assets and obligations must be separated from corporate liquidity when evaluating leverage.
- More than 1.1 million clients across over 140 countries use ADP's services, supporting scale and recurring-revenue advantages.
- The quarterly dividend declared in August 2026 was $1.70 per share, implying a $6.80 annualized dividend and an approximately 2.4% indicated yield at the reference price.
- At the reference price, fiscal 2026 diluted EPS implies a trailing P/E of approximately 25.7 times. This is an analyst calculation, not a figure reported by ADP.

## Consequential assumptions

- Payroll and HCM services remain mission-critical, recurring, and difficult enough to replace that client retention stays near its current level.
- ADP can sustain mid-single-digit organic revenue growth through a combination of bookings, retention, pays-per-control growth, product expansion, and pricing.
- Pricing and productivity gains offset wage, technology, compliance, and service costs, allowing margins to remain stable or expand modestly.
- AI improves product value and internal productivity without commoditizing payroll/HCM software or enabling materially lower-cost competitors to erode retention.
- Management continues funding dividends and net repurchases from internally generated cash rather than weakening the balance sheet.
- Interest income on client funds normalizes without causing an unexpected decline in consolidated earnings power.
- A purchase must offer at least an 8% conservative expected annual return over five years; valuation discipline is part of the thesis, not an afterthought.

## Unknowns

- ADP's normalized free cash flow after separating client-funds movements, and the resulting free-cash-flow yield at $280.83.
- A defensible intrinsic-value range under bear, base, and bull operating assumptions.
- The stock's current forward P/E, EV/EBIT, and free-cash-flow yield relative to their own 10-year distributions.
- The sensitivity of earnings to employment contraction, lower short-term interest rates, weaker bookings, and declining client-funds balances.
- Whether 92.1% retention and recent margin expansion are sustainable through a recession.
- The magnitude of stock-based compensation relative to repurchases and the long-run net reduction in diluted shares.
- Customer concentration, competitive win/loss trends, and switching behavior by client segment.
- The likelihood and financial impact of cybersecurity, privacy, regulatory, and AI-related failures.
- Whether management's fiscal 2027 outlook embeds assumptions that are more optimistic than the fund's base case.

## Research plan

1. Reconcile fiscal 2026 operating cash flow to normalized free cash flow, explicitly separating client-funds activity, acquisitions, and unusual working-capital items.
2. Build a five-year per-share history for revenue, EPS, free cash flow, dividends, repurchases, stock-based compensation, and diluted shares.
3. Construct a bear/base/bull valuation using a discounted-cash-flow model and normalized P/E and EV/EBIT exit multiples; calculate five-year expected returns at several entry prices.
4. Compare the current valuation with ADP's 10-year valuation range and with Paychex and other relevant payroll/HCM peers, adjusting for growth, retention, margins, and balance-sheet differences.
5. Stress-test revenue and EPS for recessionary employment declines, lower client-funds yields, weaker bookings, retention deterioration, and margin compression.
6. Review the 10-K risk factors, cybersecurity disclosures, debt maturities, pension/benefit obligations, capital allocation, and auditor report.
7. Review the latest earnings call and fiscal 2027 guidance; identify management claims that can be checked in subsequent quarters.
8. Set an entry-price range that meets the fund's 8% expected-return hurdle with a margin of safety, then define monitoring triggers for retention, bookings, pays per control, margin, and share count.
